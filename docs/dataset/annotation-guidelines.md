# Dataset Annotation Guidelines

## CREDIBLE

Use this label when the central claim is supported by reliable evidence.

Examples of evidence:
- official disaster/emergency information
- reliable independent reporting
- verified fact-check evidence
- multiple reliable sources

Do not mark a claim credible solely because the account appears trustworthy.


## MISLEADING

Use this label when reliable evidence demonstrates that the material claim is false
or presented in a deceptive context.

Examples:
- fabricated disaster warning
- false casualty number
- incorrect location
- old event presented as current
- unrelated image used to represent another event
- altered meaning of an official announcement


## SUSPICIOUS

Use this label where there is insufficient reliable evidence to confirm or refute
the claim at the time of annotation.

Examples:
- newly emerging event
- conflicting credible reports
- claim cannot yet be independently verified

Sensational language alone is not sufficient for this label.


## IMAGE-TEXT CONSISTENCY

consistent:
The image is semantically compatible with the textual claim.

inconsistent:
The image clearly conflicts with the textual claim.

uncertain:
Available evidence is insufficient to determine consistency.

not_applicable:
No image is attached.
